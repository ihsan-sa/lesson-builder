  <Section title="Key Ideas">
    <P>
      The relation is <M>{"v = L\\,di/dt"}</M>: the voltage across an inductor of inductance <M>{"L"}</M>
      (in henries) is proportional to the rate of change of its current, so a rapidly changing current
      produces a large voltage and a steady current produces none. In DC steady state an inductor is
      therefore a short circuit; when the current is interrupted suddenly, <M>{"di/dt"}</M> is large and
      negative and the induced voltage spikes — which is why a switch opening an inductive load arcs.
